# Brand comparison

This document explains how to read the product data and what the numbers mean. The
full tables are generated in
[`generated/brand-comparison.md`](generated/brand-comparison.md) from
`data/products.csv`.

## 1. What was measured

Sixteen products were priced on 18 November 2026 from the brand store or a named
retailer. Cost is expressed per **400 mg of elemental magnesium**, close to the adult
RDA and a common daily dose. This normalises for salt mass, serving size and pack
size, which are the three ways magnesium products hide their real cost.

## 2. The headline result

| Metric | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cost range per 400 mg | $0.08 – $2.92 |
| Spread | 37× |
| Median cost | about $0.51 |
| Cheapest | Nature's Blend Mag-Oxide, $0.08 |
| Most expensive | Life Extension Neuro-Mag (threonate), $2.92 |

A 37-fold spread for a mineral whose absorbed dose differs by a factor of two or
three at most is the clearest sign that price is tracking marketing, format and
positioning rather than efficacy.

## 3. Median cost by form

| Form | Median cost per 400 mg | Comment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Oxide | $0.10 | Cheapest, worst absorbed, most laxative |
| Malate | $0.40 | Mid-priced, unproven claims |
| Citrate | $0.46 | Best evidence-to-cost ratio |
| Glycinate chelate | $0.60 | Tolerability premium |
| Taurate | $0.78 | Cardiovascular marketing premium |
| Bisglycinate | $0.88 | Sleep/tolerability premium |
| Chloride | $1.68 | Low elemental dose, sustained-release premium |
| L-threonate | $2.92 | Cognitive hypothesis premium |

The ordering is close to the inverse of the marketing hierarchy: the cheapest form
is the least absorbed, and the most expensive is the least proven.

## 4. Value tiers

### Tier 1: cheapest per milligram

**Nature's Blend Mag-Oxide ($0.08), Mag-Ox ($0.12).** Both are 400 mg elemental
oxide per serving, cheap and effective as laxatives. They are poor choices for
repletion because oxide is only about 4% absorbed, and both exceed the 350 mg
supplemental UL per serving. Mag-Ox's label suggests two tablets daily, which would
be 800 mg — more than twice the UL.

### Tier 2: best balance

**Source Naturals Magnesium Malate ($0.29), Nutricost Magnesium Glycinate powder
($0.31), NOW Magnesium Citrate ($0.40), Doctor's Best lysinate glycinate ($0.40),
KAL Magnesium Taurate+ ($0.45).** These deliver a sensible elemental dose at a fair
price. Citrate and glycinate have the best tolerability and evidence; malate and
taurate are fine as magnesium but their specific claims are unproven.

### Tier 3: convenience and formulation premiums

**NOW Magnesium Malate ($0.51), Natural Vitality CALM ($0.51), Doctor's Best veggie
caps ($0.79), NOW Magnesium Glycinate ($0.80), Thorne Magnesium Glycinate ($0.96),
Jarrow Magnesium Taurate ($1.11).** You are paying for format, brand, chelation or
co-factors. Some of that is worth it — capsules are convenient, Thorne's labelling
is excellent — but the magnesium itself is not better.

### Tier 4: poor value

**SlowMag ($1.68), Thorne Magnesium Bisglycinate powder ($1.73), Life Extension
Neuro-Mag ($2.92).** SlowMag delivers only 143 mg per two tablets plus calcium, at a
premium. Thorne's powder is the only certified product in the sample, which may
justify part of its price. Neuro-Mag is 7–37× the cost of alternatives for a
low-dose magnesium product with unproven cognitive benefits.

## 5. What certification buys

Only one product in the sample, Thorne Magnesium Bisglycinate powder, carried a
documented certification (NSF Certified for Sport). That is a genuine quality
signal: batch-level contaminant and prohibited-substance testing. It does not make
the magnesium more effective, and it does not make a $1.73 dose better than a $0.40
one for a non-athlete.

If certification matters — for doping control, for a compromised immune system, or
for peace of mind — expect to pay a premium and check the certifier's public listing
rather than the label alone.

## 6. Serving-size traps in this sample

- **Mag-Ox** is 400 mg per tablet, with a suggested dose of two tablets (800 mg).
  The front label reads as a single dose.
- **Nature's Blend** is 400 mg per **two** tablets, so one tablet is 200 mg.
- **Source Naturals malate** is 425 mg per **three** tablets.
- **NOW malate** is 113 mg per tablet; reaching 400 mg takes 3.5 tablets.
- **Jarrow taurate** is 300 mg per **three** capsules.
- **Life Extension threonate** is 144 mg per **three** capsules.
- **SlowMag** is 143 mg per **two** tablets.

A product that looks cheap per bottle can be expensive per milligram once the
serving size is accounted for. The generated tables show servings needed for 400 mg
for exactly this reason.

## 7. Blends and co-factors

Four products in the sample contain active co-ingredients:

- **SlowMag:** calcium carbonate, 238 mg calcium per serving. Calcium and magnesium
  can compete at high doses; the combination is not inherently better.
- **Jarrow taurate:** 20 mg vitamin B6 per serving.
- **KAL taurate:** vitamin B6 plus an "ActiSorb" herbal base.
- **Natural Vitality CALM:** flavouring and sweetener only; magnesium citrate is the
  sole active.

B6 does not increase intestinal magnesium absorption. It may add a small benefit in
stressed adults with low magnesium (R26), but that is a separate effect and not a
reason to pay for a combination product. Herbal "absorption enhancers" have no
magnesium-specific evidence.

## 8. Red flags when shopping

- Front-of-pack "500 mg" that is compound weight, not elemental magnesium.
- "Magnesium complex" with no per-salt amounts.
- Doses above 350 mg per serving presented as an everyday dose.
- "99% absorption", "10× bioavailable", "crosses the blood-brain barrier" without a
  citation.
- Magnesium oil, sprays or bath flakes marketed as supplements.
- Gummies with 50–100 mg per piece and a high price per milligram.
- Subscription-only pricing that hides the one-time cost.

## 9. How to choose

1. Decide whether you actually need magnesium (see the report, section 12).
2. Pick a form for your goal: citrate or bisglycinate for general use, oxide or
   citrate for constipation, citrate for migraine (supervised), none for cognition.
3. Find the elemental dose per serving and per unit.
4. Calculate cost per 400 mg.
5. Prefer a product with a clear label and, if it matters to you, certification.
6. Start low and reassess.

## 10. What this sample cannot tell you

- Whether a specific bottle currently on a shelf matches the batch that was tested.
- Whether a brand's other products are as well labelled as the one priced.
- Which product tastes best or is easiest to swallow.
- Market-wide averages: sixteen purposive products are an illustration, not a
  survey.

Prices change. The method is reproducible; re-run the source URLs before buying.
